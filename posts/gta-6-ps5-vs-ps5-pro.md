On GTA 6, Xbox vs PS5 Pro vs base PS5 comes down to image quality, not speed. Rockstar said before launch that every console targets 30 frames per second, and the PS5 Pro's "Enhanced" extras are a sharper image through PSSR upscaling plus ray-traced shadows that no other console gets. So the Pro version should look cleaner. Based on what Rockstar announced, it shouldn't play any faster.

## What Rockstar confirmed

The Pro label on the PlayStation Store came first, and for a while that's all anyone had. Rockstar filled in the details on November 10, nine days before launch. Here's what it said, console by console:

- **All consoles:** a 30 fps target on PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S.
- **PS5 Pro only:** improved clarity through PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR), and ray-traced shadows.
- **All PS5 models:** DualSense haptic feedback (adjustable in accessibility settings), Tempest 3D Audio and improved load times.
- **Xbox One:** no native version, but you can stream it through Xbox Cloud Gaming using Series S graphics settings.

Rockstar and PlayStation also repeated their marketing line that the game plays best on PS5. That's part of a marketing deal, so read it as a sales pitch. The concrete differences are the controller features and the Pro's image extras listed above.

## What the Enhanced label means on PS5 Pro

Before November 10, the store's PS5 Pro Enhanced badge only told you Rockstar had done some Pro-specific work. It didn't say what. Now we know the work is about image quality:

- **PSSR** is Sony's upscaler. It takes a lower internal resolution and rebuilds a sharper output image. In practice that means less shimmer on fine detail like fences, palm fronds and power lines.
- **Ray-traced shadows** are more accurate shadows, softer and better placed, especially under trees, cars and awnings.

What Rockstar didn't promise is a higher frame rate. It didn't announce a 60 fps mode or a 40 fps mode on any console. If one ever arrives, it will show up in Rockstar's patch notes, so check those rather than forum claims.

## Frame rate and resolution: what's still unannounced

Rockstar hasn't given resolution targets for any console. Even the 30 fps figure is a target, which isn't the same as a locked 30 at all times. Analysts like Digital Foundry have pointed out that the PS5 Pro's CPU is close to the base PS5's, and CPU limits are a common reason open-world games stay at 30. That's an outside explanation, not Rockstar's.

For how the finished game actually runs on each console, look for side-by-side tests from outlets that measure performance. Before launch, the best look at the game was the 27-minute Extended Look, which was captured on a standard PS5 and appeared to run at about 30 fps for most of its runtime.

## GTA 6 on Xbox Series X vs PS5 Pro

Same target, different features. The Series X and the base PS5 sit at the same 30 fps target without the Pro's PSSR or ray-traced shadows. Rockstar didn't announce Xbox-only graphics features. The Series S targets 30 fps too, and its settings are the ones used for cloud streaming.

The other difference is the controller. On PS5, PlayStation's trailer showed weapon recoil through the triggers, extra pressure during weightlifting, and police radio chatter coming out of the DualSense speaker. Xbox controllers don't have those features. If you don't care about haptics, that gap won't matter to you.

## Storage on each console

The download was up to 212 GB, depending on the console. That's the starting point, and patches tend to add more. How it fits:

- **PS5 Pro:** ships with a 2 TB drive, so it has the most room of the three.
- **Base PS5:** fits, but you may have to clear older games.
- **Xbox Series S:** about 364 GB of usable space, so GTA 6 takes most of it.

Both PS5 and Series X|S accept extra storage if you run short. See our [file size page](https://callunc.com/file-size/) for how to check free space.

## Should you buy a PS5 Pro for GTA 6?

If you already own a base PS5 or a Series X, you can play the same game at the same frame rate target. The Pro buys you a cleaner image and nicer shadows. That's a real improvement on a big 4K TV, and a small one on a smaller screen.

A Pro makes the most sense if you were already planning to upgrade, you sit close to a large TV, or you're short on storage. It makes the least sense if you're hoping for 60 fps, because Rockstar hasn't announced one.

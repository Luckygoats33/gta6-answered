You can't play GTA 6 from a USB external hard drive on either console. A USB drive can hold the game while you're not playing it, but to actually run GTA 6 it has to sit on the console's internal storage or on the fast expansion storage Sony and Microsoft support: an M.2 SSD on PS5, or a Storage Expansion Card on Xbox Series X|S.

GTA 6 is a current-generation-only game, and both consoles treat their current-generation games the same way: they need fast internal-class storage to run.

## Why GTA 6 storage is a problem in the first place

Rockstar puts the download at up to 212 GB, depending on your console. That's before any patches, which tend to make big games bigger over time. On a console that's also holding a couple of other modern games, the space disappears fast.

Rough usable space on the common consoles, as widely reported:

- **Original PS5 (825 GB):** about 667 GB usable.
- **PS5 Slim (1 TB):** roughly 840 to 850 GB usable.
- **PS5 Pro (2 TB):** close to 1.9 TB usable.
- **Xbox Series X (1 TB):** about 802 GB usable.
- **Xbox Series S (512 GB):** about 364 GB usable.

The Series S is the tight one. GTA 6 alone takes a big share of it. Your own console's storage screen is the only number that counts, so check there first: on PS5 it's Settings, Storage, Console Storage, and on Xbox it's Settings, System, Storage devices.

## PS5: USB storage vs an M.2 SSD

Sony's support pages are clear about the split.

**USB extended storage** can store PS5 games, but you can't play them from it. To play, you copy the game back to console storage. Sony's requirements for the drive:

- 250 GB to 8 TB.
- A SuperSpeed USB connection of 5 Gbps or faster.

**An M.2 SSD** installed inside the console does let you play PS5 games directly. Sony's requirements:

- 250 GB to 8 TB.
- PCIe Gen4 x4 NVMe (M.2 SATA drives aren't supported).
- 5,500 MB/s read speed or faster recommended.
- A heatsink or heat transfer sheet, either built in or added.
- Update the console's system software before installing it.

Sony also notes that some games might not perform exactly as they do on internal storage, and you can move a game back if you hit problems. For a game the size of GTA 6, the M.2 slot is the real way to expand PS5 storage.

## Xbox Series X|S: USB vs the Storage Expansion Card

Microsoft draws the same line. A USB 3.1 external drive can play older Xbox One, Xbox 360 and original Xbox games, but it can't play games built for Series X|S. Those need the internal SSD or the official Storage Expansion Card, which Microsoft designed to match the internal drive's performance.

So on Xbox:

- **USB drive:** fine for storing GTA 6 (or parking other Series X|S games) and copying them back later.
- **Storage Expansion Card:** plugs into the back of the console and runs GTA 6 like internal storage.

If you're on a Series S, our [Series S post](https://callunc.com/blog/gta-6-xbox-series-s/) goes deeper into making the space work.

## The cheap trick: use USB as a parking lot

If you don't want to buy fast storage, a regular USB drive still helps. The idea is to keep GTA 6 on the internal drive and move everything else out of its way.

1. Plug in a USB drive that meets your console's requirements.
2. Move the games you aren't playing to it. On PS5, use Move Games and Apps from the Game Library. On Xbox, use Manage game and add-ons, then move or copy.
3. When you want one of those games back, copy it to internal storage again.

Copying back over USB is much faster than downloading a game from scratch, which is the whole point. With a 212 GB game, avoiding a full redownload saves real time.

## What to buy, if you buy anything

- **You play a few big games at once:** fast expansion storage (an M.2 on PS5, an expansion card on Xbox) is the clean answer.
- **You mostly play one game at a time:** a USB drive as a parking lot is usually enough.
- **You're on Series S and hate deleting:** the expansion card is the upgrade that actually lets you play more off the console at once.

Before you buy, check the console's own storage screen, check the drive against Sony's or Microsoft's requirements, and be wary of "PS5 compatible" labels on cheap drives that don't list the specs.

The short version of GTA 6 vs RDR2: GTA 6 has the bigger map and the modern, faster world, while Red Dead Redemption 2 remains Rockstar's slow, heavy, one-man story. They come from the same studio and share a lot of DNA, but they want different things from you. GTA 6 came out on November 19, 2026, so this compares Rockstar's own statements about it with a game that has been out since 2018.

## Map size compared

Rockstar has been direct about this one. Development head Aaron Garbut said Leonida is roughly twice the size of GTA V's map, and Rockstar said its total landmass is close to the playable areas of GTA IV, GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 combined. So RDR2's map, on Rockstar's own framing, is only part of what GTA 6 covers.

There's no official square-mile figure for either game, so treat any precise ratio you see as fan math. Some outlets have said GTA 6 is about three times RDR2. That's not a number I'd repeat as fact.

The feel of the space is different too. RDR2's map is wilderness with a handful of towns and one city, Saint Denis. GTA 6 flips that: a big modern city in Vice City, plus swamps, islands, a run-down port town, farm country and a national park around it. ## World detail

RDR2 earned its reputation on small details, from the gang's camp life to the way the world reacts to how you behave. Rockstar's pitch for GTA 6 is the same obsession, applied to a modern state. Garbut said the team obsessed over every part of the world.

From what Rockstar showed before launch, the detail shows up in new places:

- **Police that track you by look, not just location.** The Netflix Extended Look showed a search area on the map and showed that changing masks, hats or clothes can bring the heat down.
- **Social media inside the world.** The footage showed an influencer livestreaming from Lucia's car with a chat on screen.
- **Water as a real playground.** Fan boats, kayaks and boats around the Keys and Grassrivers.

Rockstar also described a weather system modeled on Florida's, including strong storms, in its Game Informer coverage, as Dexerto reported. How all of that holds up across a full playthrough is something reviews and your own hours will answer, not a trailer.

## Two protagonists vs one

This is the biggest story difference.

- **RDR2** follows Arthur Morgan, a member of the Van der Linde gang, in 1899. John Marston takes over in the epilogue. It's a long, sad, mostly linear character study.
- **GTA 6** follows Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a pair who work together. The Extended Look showed missions you can play as either, and moments where one is inside a store while the other drives getaway.

GTA V had three leads you could switch between. GTA 6 has two, but sells them as a partnership rather than three separate stories that cross. If RDR2's single, deep point of view is what you loved, GTA 6's split focus will feel different. Whether that's better is taste.

## Open world activities

RDR2 is about hunting, fishing, camp chores, poker, bounties and the honor system that rewarded or punished how you behaved. GTA 6 swaps the frontier routine for modern life. From Rockstar's material:

- Mount Kalaga National Park is pitched for hunting, fishing and off-road trails, which sounds closest to RDR2
- The Extended Look showed dirt bike races, kayaking, parachuting and a gym
- Vice City brings the classic GTA stuff: cars, shops, robberies and police chases

GTA 6 doesn't advertise an honor system like RDR2's. Rockstar hasn't described one, so don't assume it exists.

## Which to play first

If you haven't played either and you own a PS5 or Xbox Series X|S, I'd play GTA 6 first. It's the new one, it's the one everyone is talking about, and its faster pace is easier to fall into. RDR2 asks for patience, and it rewards it best when you're in the mood for a slow story.

If you've already finished RDR2, go in expecting a different kind of game. GTA has always been louder and funnier, and Rockstar's own description of Leonida leans into satire.

If you're on PS4, the choice is made for you: RDR2 plays there, GTA 6 doesn't. Xbox One is a half exception. GTA 6 has no Xbox One version, but if you own it you can stream it to an Xbox One through Xbox Cloud Gaming.

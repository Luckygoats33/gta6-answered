The best pre-launch evidence of GTA 6 quality on a console is the 27-minute Extended Look, which Netflix says was captured entirely in-game on PlayStation 5, and Engadget notes it was a standard PS5, not a Pro. It showed detailed lighting, faces and crowds at what looked like about 30 frames per second. Rockstar's own numbers line up with that: every console targets 30 fps, and only the PS5 Pro gets extra image features. Here's what the official footage showed, and what Rockstar did and didn't say about the graphics.

## What the Extended Look showed on PS5

The Extended Look premiered on Netflix on August 27, 2026, then went up on Rockstar's YouTube channel. It's the longest stretch of real gameplay Rockstar released before launch, so it's the fairest source for judging the graphics, with two caveats.

First, the Netflix stream was low bitrate. Engadget pointed out that this made fine detail hard to judge. Second, Rockstar chose what to show. Any studio's showcase is the game on its best behavior.

With that said, the footage showed a lot. In-game scenes, not cutscenes, included cover shooting, chases with a six-star wanted level, shop robberies, fan boat rides and base-jumping.

## Lighting and weather

Lighting is where GTA 6 looks most different from GTA V. In the Extended Look, light changes how surfaces read from scene to scene. Engadget picked out light filtering through a character's dreads and catching on gold jewelry, and the stitching and fabric texture on an embroidered leather jacket. Neon, headlights and sunsets do a lot of the work in Vice City at night.

Weather is new territory for the series. Rockstar told Game Informer about an advanced weather system, including hurricanes and strong wind gusts that affect the people on the street. Rockstar hasn't said how often storms hit or whether they change missions.

## Crowds and traffic

GTA 6's streets look busier than anything Rockstar has shipped. The footage showed security guards, people hanging around apartment blocks, nightclub crowds and sunbathers by the water, plus busy roads.

What Rockstar has said about the people in the world: they come in a wider range of body types and looks than in past games, and the team researched real Florida to build its parody of it. Leonida also has more than 170 animal species. Rockstar hasn't published crowd sizes or a pedestrian count, so treat any number you see online as a guess.

## Faces and animation

Faces are the clearest jump. Lucia and Jason hold up in close-ups, and small touches carry a lot. Lucia's hair changes from scene to scene in the Extended Look, from braids to a messy bun to wigs. In combat, animations flow into each other: Engadget noted Lucia tossing and catching a shotgun mid-reload and using her heel in a melee fight.

If you want the "GTA 6 character models" close-ups, the Launch Trailer from October 2026, also captured on PS5, is the other good source. Our [trailers post](https://callunc.com/blog/gta-6-trailers/) lists every official video in order.

## Console differences

Rockstar's November 10 announcement settled the main questions:

- **Frame rate:** 30 fps is the target on PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S.
- **PS5 Pro:** sharper image through PSSR upscaling, and ray-traced shadows that are only on the Pro.
- **Xbox One:** there's no Xbox One version, but if you own the game you can stream it to an Xbox One through Xbox Cloud Gaming, at Series S settings.
- **Resolution:** Rockstar hasn't given targets for any console.

The game came out on November 19, 2026. For how it actually looks and runs on each machine, look for side-by-side tests from outlets that measure performance, and check Rockstar's patch notes for any graphics changes after launch. Before launch, Rockstar didn't announce a performance mode or a 60 fps option.

## Do you need a PS5 Pro or a big TV?

No. The base PS5 is what the Extended Look was captured on, and it's the footage most people think of when they say GTA 6 looks great. The Pro's extras are about clarity and shadows, which you'll notice most on a large 4K screen. The Series X and Series S get the same game at the same frame rate target.

The one thing that will hurt image quality more than your console is a bad TV setting. Turn off motion smoothing, use your TV's game mode, and run the console's own HDR calibration.

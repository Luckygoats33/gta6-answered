There are no official GTA 6 settings requirements for PC, because Rockstar hasn't announced a PC version. GTA 6 comes out on November 19, 2026 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only. Any chart showing "GTA 6 minimum, high and ultra specs" is a guess dressed up as a fact.

What we can do is look at how Rockstar handled its last two PC releases, and what that suggests about the hardware a PC version would eventually ask for.

## No official GTA 6 expected system requirements yet

As of November 16, 2026, Rockstar's platform list for GTA 6 names two platforms: PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. There's no PC release date, no PC store page and no spec sheet. Our [PC page](https://callunc.com/pc/) has the one-line status, and the [PC release post](https://callunc.com/blog/gta-6-pc-release/) covers the timing guesses.

That means nobody knows:

- the minimum or recommended graphics card,
- how much RAM it needs,
- whether it requires ray tracing hardware,
- the install size on PC,
- or which settings would count as "high" or "ultra".

Sites that publish those charts are usually extrapolating from the console hardware, or just making them up. Some are fronts for fake download links. Treat them that way.

## What RDR2 and GTA V needed on PC

History is the only honest guide. Rockstar's last two big PC ports both came out well after their console versions, and both published official requirements shortly before launch.

### Red Dead Redemption 2 (PC, November 2019)

RDR2 hit consoles in October 2018 and PC about a year later. Rockstar's official specs, as published at the time:

- **Minimum:** Intel Core i5-2500K or AMD FX-6300, 8 GB RAM, Nvidia GTX 770 2 GB or AMD Radeon R9 280 3 GB, 150 GB of storage.
- **Recommended:** Intel Core i7-4770K or AMD Ryzen 5 1500X, 12 GB RAM, Nvidia GTX 1060 6 GB or AMD Radeon RX 480 4 GB, 150 GB of storage.

Those weren't brutal numbers for the time. The storage figure was the one that made headlines.

### GTA V (PC, 2015, and Enhanced, 2025)

GTA V reached PC in April 2015, about a year and a half after the 2013 console launch. The original PC requirements were modest:

- **Minimum:** Intel Core 2 Quad Q6600 or AMD Phenom 9850, Nvidia 9800 GT 1 GB or AMD HD 4870 1 GB.
- **Recommended:** Intel Core i5-3470 or AMD FX-8350, Nvidia GTX 660 2 GB or AMD HD 7870 2 GB.

In March 2025 Rockstar released an Enhanced PC version with ray tracing features, and its list moved up: a GTX 1630 or RX 6400 (4 GB) at minimum, and an RTX 3060 or RX 6600 XT (8 GB) recommended.

The pattern: Rockstar's PC requirements have tracked the console generation the game was built for, plus a margin. GTA 6 was built for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, so if a PC version arrives, it's reasonable to expect it to ask for hardware at least in that class. That's a reasonable inference, not a spec.

## Why storage will matter

If you take one thing from this post, make it this one. On console, GTA 6 is a download of up to 212 GB. A PC version would almost certainly be large too, and modern open-world games increasingly expect an SSD, not a spinning hard drive.

What that means in practice:

- Budget for a fast NVMe SSD with room to spare, not just enough for one install.
- Remember patches usually grow a game over time.
- Don't count on an old hard drive to keep up with streaming a dense city like Vice City.

RDR2 asked for 150 GB in 2019. GTA 6's console download is already bigger than that.

## What to wait for before upgrading

Buying a graphics card "for GTA 6 on PC" now means guessing at a release that hasn't been announced. A more sensible order:

1. **Wait for Rockstar to announce a PC version.** That's the only trigger that matters.
2. **Wait for the official requirements.** Rockstar published both RDR2's and GTA V's specs ahead of their PC launches.
3. **Wait for independent testing.** Outlets like PC Gamer benchmark big ports at release, and the real performance story often differs from the spec sheet.
4. **Then buy for the resolution you actually play at.** A 1080p player and a 4K player need very different cards.

If you want to play GTA 6 at launch, the answer is a PS5 or an Xbox Series console. If you're set on PC, the cheapest upgrade is patience. Hardware gets better and cheaper while you wait, and the spec sheet will tell you exactly what to buy.

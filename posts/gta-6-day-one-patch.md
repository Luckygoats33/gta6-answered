If you're looking for the latest GTA 6 update, the honest answer is that Rockstar's own patch notes are the only place to get the details, and before launch Rockstar didn't announce a day one patch, its size, or what it would fix. GTA 6 came out on November 19, 2026. What you can control is making sure your console grabs every update on its own, and knowing where to look when something changes.

## What Rockstar said before launch

Not much, and that's normal for Rockstar. Here is what was on the record going in:

- **The download size.** Rockstar put the pre-load at up to 212 GB, depending on the console. That figure covered the base game. Rockstar didn't publish a separate size for any launch update.
- **Frame rate.** On November 10, Rockstar confirmed a 30 fps target on PS5, PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X|S, with PSSR upscaling and ray-traced shadows added on PS5 Pro. Earlier, Rob Nelson, Rockstar's head of development, had said in an interview that the consoles were running the game at 30 frames per second "for now", and that a 60 fps option was a question for the technical team. That is not a promise of a performance mode in a future update. No 60 fps mode was announced before release.
- **Online.** GTA 6 launched single-player. Rockstar hadn't given a date for GTA 6 Online, or said how it will be delivered.

Everything else you'll see about "the GTA 6 launch update" on social media in the first week is either someone reading their own console's download queue or a guess. Before launch, a few outlets reported store backend numbers that didn't match the 212 GB figure and floated patch sizes from 15 GB to 50 GB. None of those came from Rockstar, so treat them as noise.

## How to check for a new GTA 6 update on PS5

1. Go to your Game Library and highlight GTA 6.
2. Press the Options button.
3. Choose **Check for Update**.

To make it automatic: Settings, Saved Data and Game/App Settings, Automatic Updates. Turn on **Auto-Download**, and also **Auto-Install in Rest Mode** so the console installs while it's sleeping. Rest mode needs to be allowed to stay connected to the internet, which you'll find under Settings, System, Power Saving, Features Available in Rest Mode.

## How to check for updates on Xbox Series X|S

1. Press the Xbox button and go to **My games and apps**.
2. Highlight GTA 6, press the Menu button, and choose **Manage game and add-ons**.
3. Open **Updates**. Anything waiting will be listed there.

For automatic updates: Settings, System, Updates, and turn on keeping your games and apps up to date. The console also needs to be in the sleep power mode, not full shutdown, if you want it to download while you're away.

## Make room before it's needed

This is the part that bites people. An update needs free space to download and install, sometimes more than its final size while it unpacks. If your drive is full after a 200 GB install, the update just sits there and the game may refuse to start until it's done.

- **On PS5,** check Settings, Storage. If you need space, Console Storage, Games and Apps lets you delete things, and some games let you delete only parts of their data.
- **On Xbox,** Settings, System, Storage devices shows what's left.

A rough rule I use: keep a healthy chunk of the drive free for the first month of any huge game. Our [file size page](https://callunc.com/file-size/) has more on how big GTA 6 is per console.

## Where real patch notes show up

When Rockstar ships an update, the details usually land in two places:

- **Rockstar Support**, which posts title update notes for its games.
- **The Rockstar Newswire**, for anything bigger, like new content.

Your console will also show a version number for the game. On PS5, press Options on the game and look at Information. On Xbox, it's under Manage game and add-ons. If a post on social media claims a patch "secretly added" something, check it against Rockstar's notes before you believe it.

## Should you wait for patches before playing?

That's a personal call. Some people like to play a big release on day one and live with whatever rough edges it has. Others wait a week or two for the first fixes. Rockstar hadn't told anyone what its launch plan for updates looked like, so there's no official reason to wait or to rush.

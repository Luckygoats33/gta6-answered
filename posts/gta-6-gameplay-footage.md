The most GTA 6 gameplay Rockstar showed before release is "Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look", a roughly 27-minute video captured on PS5 that premiered on Netflix on August 27, 2026 and then went up on Rockstar's YouTube channel. Before that, the second trailer in May 2025 was the first official footage Rockstar described as part gameplay.

The game itself came out on November 19, 2026, so the best footage now is whatever you play. But if you want to know what Rockstar chose to show, and what it didn't, here's the record.

## Where to watch official GTA 6 gameplay

Stick to Rockstar's channels and the partners it named. Everything else is a reupload, a reaction video or, at worst, the 2022 leak, which is old development material and not what shipped.

1. **Trailer 1 (December 2023).** The reveal. It set up Leonida, Vice City and Lucia, and broke YouTube viewing records, but it was a trailer, not a gameplay demo.
2. **Trailer 2 (May 2025).** Rockstar said it was captured entirely on PS5 and made of equal parts gameplay and cutscenes. This is where you first see Jason and Lucia together in motion.
3. **An Extended Look (August 2026).** The long one. Netflix had it first (Engadget says for about six hours) before it went to YouTube and other platforms. Netflix lists it as 27 minutes, captured on PS5.

Our [Netflix page](https://callunc.com/netflix/) explains why that premiere confused people into thinking the game was playable there. It never was.

## What the Extended Look covered

The Extended Look is the closest thing to a gameplay demo Rockstar released. Here's what outlets picked out of it.

### Driving and the open world

- High-speed police chases, including a getaway in a red convertible where control passes from Jason to Lucia mid-chase.
- Car theft that's more involved than pressing a button. Engadget points to a "Slim Jim" moment where Jason works a lock with a timed prompt, and reports Rob Nelson telling IGN that higher-end cars need key cloners.
- A map overlay where buildings appear in 3D, which should make Vice City's dense streets easier to read.
- Side activities spotted across the video, including basketball, dirt bike races, scuba diving, target practice, stock car racing and kayaking.

### Combat

- Swapping between two weapons on the fly without opening the weapon wheel.
- Picking up enemy guns quickly during firefights.
- One-handed use of some weapons, such as Lucia firing a shotgun while carrying a bag.
- A slow-motion aim with markers on enemies, which Engadget reads as lethal and non-lethal target points.

### Police and consequences

- **Six Wanted stars** are back, after GTA V capped out at five.
- A new **Criminal Profile** system, described in IGN's preview (as reported by Engadget), which judges crimes by context rather than treating every one the same.

### Two protagonists

The video shows switching between Jason and Lucia, but not much of it. Nelson later told Famitsu (reported by PC Gamer) that switching depends on the mission: some let you swap freely, some swap for you, and some keep you in one character's shoes.

## What hasn't been shown

Rockstar was selective, as it always is. Before launch it hadn't shown:

- **Anything about online.** Rockstar hadn't announced a GTA 6 online mode when we checked on November 16, 2026. Our [online page](https://callunc.com/online/) has the status.
- **Performance modes.** Rockstar later confirmed a 30 fps target on every console, PS5 Pro included, with PSSR upscaling and ray-traced shadows on the Pro. It hasn't announced a 60 fps or performance mode, and the footage itself was PS5 capture, which tells you nothing about Xbox.
- **A full map.** Rockstar named six regions and described them, but didn't publish a complete map. The [map size page](https://callunc.com/map-size/) covers what it did say.
- **PC.** There's still no PC version announced, so any "PC gameplay" video is either PS5 footage relabeled or fake.

## Spotting fake GTA 6 gameplay

Search "GTA 6 gameplay" and you'll find a lot of things that aren't. Common types:

- GTA V with mods and a new logo.
- AI-generated "leaks" that look slightly wrong if you watch the hands and the license plates.
- The 2022 leak footage, which shows an early build and isn't the released game.

If the channel isn't Rockstar's, or the video isn't from a reputable outlet covering an official release, treat it as entertainment, not evidence.

## The footage that matters now

With the game out, the pre-release videos are mostly a time capsule: what Rockstar wanted you to see first. They're worth watching for that. For anything about how the game plays after patches, look to Rockstar's own patch notes and support pages, not old trailers.

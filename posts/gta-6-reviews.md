GTA 6 reviews are rumored to go live on November 18, 2026, the day before the game comes out on November 19. That date came from a leaker and was backed up by Insider Gaming, not announced by Rockstar, so treat it as a rumor rather than official. As of November 16, no reviews are out yet. The useful question is where to read them when they land and how to tell a real one from the noise.

The game isn't out yet, so you won't find scores here. We don't make up numbers, and we'd rather send you to the places that actually publish them.

## What was reported about the GTA 6 review embargo

A review embargo is the date and time a publisher lets critics publish. Here is what is known so far:

- In late October 2026, the leaker NateTheHate posted that Rockstar had started sending review code to media outlets.
- Insider Gaming said it could independently verify that the GTA 6 review embargo would lift on November 18.
- Rockstar's own Grand Theft Auto VI page lists the November 19 release date and its Mature rating, but not a review date.

None of that came from Rockstar's mouth. It's the kind of report that usually turns out right, but "usually" is doing some work in that sentence.

## Where to read reviews of GTA 6

There are two types of place to look, and you want both.

**Review aggregators.** These collect many reviews and average them.

- **Metacritic** gives a weighted average called the Metascore, plus a separate user score. The GTA 6 Metacritic page is split by console, so check the PS5 and Xbox Series X entries separately.
- **OpenCritic** shows a "Top Critic Average" and the percentage of critics who recommend the game. It's a good second opinion on the same pile of reviews.

**Individual outlets.** A single review of GTA 6 from a writer you trust tells you more than an average does. IGN, GameSpot, Eurogamer, Polygon, PC Gamer and GamesRadar all review big Rockstar games. Pick two or three and read past the score.

## How to read GTA 6 reviews like a grown-up

A score is a summary of one person's week with the game. A few things to keep in mind:

1. **Check which console they played on.** Most review code is played on PS5 or PS5 Pro. Rockstar has said that the game targets 30 frames per second on every console, with ray-traced shadows and PSSR upscaling as PS5 Pro extras. If you play on Xbox Series S, a Pro review won't tell you everything about your experience.
2. **Look for how far they got.** A huge open world takes a long time. Good reviewers say whether they finished the story and how much side content they touched.
3. **Separate the story from the systems.** Some critics care most about the writing, some about driving and shooting, some about the world. Find the reviewer whose priorities match yours.
4. **Watch for patch notes.** Reviews describe the build the critic played. If a review mentions bugs, check Rockstar's patch notes to see whether a later update addressed them.
5. **There's no GTA 6 Online to review yet.** The game launches single-player. Rockstar hasn't given a date for Online (see our [online answer](https://callunc.com/online/)), so any review that grades multiplayer is guessing.

## Fake scores and user-review bombs

Big launches attract fake "leaked review scores" and screenshots of Metascores that never existed. Before the embargo lifts, there is nothing real to leak, so anything you see before reviews go live claiming to be the GTA 6 Metacritic number is made up.

User scores are a different problem. They open to anyone who signs up, and a big game can get flooded with zeros or tens within hours, often about things like price rather than the game. Read the written user reviews, not just the number, and give it a few weeks to settle.

## For context: Rockstar's track record

Rockstar's last few big games reviewed very well. On Metacritic, GTA IV sits at 98, and GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 sit at 97. That's history, not a prediction. Every game earns its own score, and GTA 6 will too.

## Should reviews change whether you buy it?

If you've already pre-ordered, reviews are just reading material. If you're on the fence, they're worth a look, mostly for the details: how it runs on your console, how long the story is, and whether the things you care about are any good.

The best settings for GTA 6 mostly live outside the game: put your TV in Game Mode, calibrate HDR once at the console level, turn on VRR and ALLM if your TV supports them, and make sure the console is plugged into the right HDMI port. Those four steps matter more than any slider you'll find in a pause menu. GTA 6 comes out on November 19, 2026, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, and a game this bright and busy will show off a badly set TV fast. Neon at night and Leonida sun at noon will either look great or look like a smeared mess.

A note on what we won't do here: we won't invent in-game menus or performance modes. Rockstar has confirmed a 30 fps target on PS5, PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X|S, and has announced no 60 fps or performance mode. The store pages say the game is "PS5 Pro Enhanced" on PlayStation and "Optimized for Xbox Series X|S" on Xbox. So this guide covers the parts that are documented by Sony and Microsoft, which are also the parts that make the biggest difference.

## Step 1: GTA 6 TV settings start with Game Mode

Every modern TV has a low-latency picture mode, usually called Game Mode or something close to it. It turns off most of the image processing that adds delay between your controller and the screen. In a game where you're driving at speed through traffic, that delay is the difference between threading a gap and eating a palm tree.

While you're in the TV menu:

- **Turn off motion smoothing.** It goes by different brand names, but it's the setting that makes everything look like a soap opera. It adds lag and artifacts.
- **Turn off extra sharpening and "dynamic contrast."** They crush detail in shadows and blow out highlights.
- **Use the right HDMI port.** On many TVs, only some HDMI ports support 4K at 120Hz and VRR. Your TV manual will say which. If 120Hz options never show up on your console, this is usually why.

## Step 2: GTA 6 HDR, calibrated once

HDR is where most people go wrong. The fix is to calibrate at the console level, so the console knows what your TV can actually display.

### On PS5

Go to Settings, Screen and Video, Video Output. You'll find:

- **HDR:** Choose "On When Supported." That keeps non-HDR content in SDR. "Always On" forces everything into HDR, which can make some apps look off.
- **Adjust HDR:** This runs the brightness test screens. Follow the on-screen prompts on each test screen. Do it with the TV already in Game Mode, since changing the picture mode afterward throws the calibration off.
- **Deep Color Output:** Leave it on Automatic unless you see problems.

### On Xbox Series X|S

Go to Settings, General, TV & Display Options. Under Setup there's the HDR calibration for games, which walks you through test patterns. Microsoft says games can use those stored values, so it's worth doing once properly.

### A good rule of thumb

If GTA 6 has its own brightness or HDR slider, use it last and lightly, after the console calibration. Don't push brightness up to see in dark scenes. Night in Vice City is supposed to be dark.

## Step 3: VRR, ALLM and 120Hz

These three settings make motion smoother and input snappier on TVs that support them.

- **VRR (variable refresh rate)** lets your TV's refresh rate follow the game's frame rate, which cuts down on stutter and tearing when the frame rate dips. On PS5 it's in Video Output, with an extra "Apply to Unsupported Games" option. Sony says to turn that off if you see odd behavior. On Xbox, look under Advanced, Video modes for "Allow variable refresh rate."
- **ALLM (auto low latency mode)** tells the TV to switch to Game Mode automatically when you start playing. On PS5, Sony notes that low-latency mode stays on while VRR is active even with ALLM off. On Xbox, it's in Video modes too.
- **120Hz output** only does something when the game itself supports a high frame rate. Turning it on doesn't hurt, but Rockstar's target for GTA 6 is 30 fps and it hasn't announced a 120Hz mode, so don't expect a miracle. On PS5 it's "120 Hz Output." On Xbox it's under Refresh rate.

If you see flicker or a black screen after changing these, Sony suggests trying a lower Video Transfer Rate setting (the -1 or -2 options) on PS5.

## Step 4: resolution and the rest

- **PS5 Resolution:** Leave it on Automatic. It picks the highest resolution your TV supports.
- **Xbox:** Check Settings, General, TV & Display Options, 4K TV details. It tells you plainly which features your TV and HDMI cable support.
- **Cables:** Use the HDMI cable that came with the console, especially for 4K at 120Hz.

## What about PS5 Pro and graphics modes?

PlayStation lists GTA 6 as PS5 Pro Enhanced, and Rockstar explained what that means on November 10: Rockstar's target is 30 fps on every console, PS5 Pro included, and the Pro adds PSSR upscaling and ray-traced shadows. Rockstar didn't disclose rendering resolution, and no 60 fps or performance mode was announced (a 40 fps mode is only a datamine rumor). From November 19, check the game's own settings menu and Rockstar's patch notes for anything newer, and see our [consoles page](https://callunc.com/consoles/) and [facts page](https://callunc.com/facts/) for what's confirmed. If someone online is quoting a 60 fps mode, ask where they got it.

## The short checklist

1. TV in Game Mode, motion smoothing off.
2. Console in the right HDMI port with the original cable.
3. HDR calibrated on the console, with the TV already in Game Mode.
4. VRR and ALLM on if your TV supports them.
5. In-game brightness adjusted last, gently.

That's it. Ten minutes of setup, and the sunsets look the way Rockstar intended.

Rockstar's pitch for GTA 6 crash physics and driving was simple: the weight of GTA IV with the friendliness of GTA V. That's how Rob Nelson, co-head of Rockstar North, described it before launch, and the official footage backs up the "weight" part. Cars lean, slide and roll in a way GTA V's rarely did. The game came out on November 19, 2026, so you can feel it for yourself. This post sticks to what Rockstar showed and said before release, so you know which parts were promised and which were just fan hopes.

## What Rockstar said about the driving

Most of what's on the record comes from one interview. In an interview with YouTuber TGG, reported in September, Nelson said:

- The handling is weighty "in the best way possible," his answer when asked directly if the cars felt heavy.
- The goal was to keep GTA IV's sense of weight while keeping GTA V's accessibility.
- The vehicle physics were rebuilt from scratch for current consoles.
- The team has finer control over tuning the drivetrain, tires, suspension and steering than before.

Separately, Nelson told Dazed that Rockstar's vehicle handling team includes real race-car drivers. That's a nice detail, and it explains the focus on how cars carry speed through corners instead of just top speed.

If you played GTA IV, you remember what "weight" means there: cars that dip under braking, wallow over bumps and punish you for yanking the wheel at 90. GTA V went the other way, toward arcade grip that let anyone hold a line. GTA 6 is aiming for the middle. How close it lands is something you'll judge with a controller, not from a trailer.

## GTA 6 crash damage in the footage

The Extended Look, which Netflix lists at 27 minutes and which Rockstar captured entirely on PS5, is the best official source. Here's what it and the coverage around it showed:

- **Rollovers.** When tires get shot out, cars can tip over and roll several times before they stop, or simply spin out of control. That's a big step from GTA V, where most cars stayed stubbornly upright.
- **A sense of speed.** Reporters who watched the footage noted how fast the driving looks, which matters more than any single crash.
- **Vehicle condition on screen.** The HUD includes icons for the health of the two leads, their outfits, and the condition of the car you're in. So damage is tracked as something you have to manage, not just paint.
- **Chases.** A chase involving a red convertible and on-the-fly switching between Jason and Lucia showed the handling under pressure.

## What wasn't shown or confirmed

This is where a lot of videos get ahead of themselves. Before launch, Rockstar didn't confirm:

- **Soft-body deformation** in the style of BeamNG, where the car's frame crumples realistically. The footage shows cars taking damage, but Rockstar never described a full soft-body system.
- **Detailed injury from crashes** for the player characters.
- **Specific features** like detachable parts, glass shattering patterns, or fuel leaks. You may see some of this in the game. It wasn't announced.

If a clip claims "GTA 6 has the most realistic crash physics ever," check whether it's official footage, someone's gameplay, or a fan-made render. A lot of what circulated before launch was the last one.

## GTA 6 car physics beyond the crashes

A few other driving changes came up in the official material:

- **Car theft is no longer one button.** The Extended Look showed a slim-jim style minigame to break into older cars. Fancier cars with electronic locks need a key cloner, so you can't hot-wire everything.
- **Navigation got a 3D map.** Engadget noted the new HUD map shows buildings in 3D, which makes threading through traffic easier.
- **Passenger seat play.** Nelson told Dazed you can switch between driving and riding as passenger on the way to a job, and use your phone from the passenger seat.

For the cars themselves, the official list is still short. We keep what's actually been confirmed on the [facts page](https://callunc.com/facts/), and anything unproven goes on the [rumors page](https://callunc.com/rumors/).

## How to judge it yourself

Now that the game is out, the fair test is simple. Take a heavy car and a light one down the same road, brake hard into a corner, and hit something at speed. You'll know within ten minutes whether it feels like GTA IV, GTA V, or something new.

And if you're new to the series, don't fight it. Weighty handling rewards braking before the turn instead of during it. It's the oldest driving advice there is, and it works in Leonida too.

The game's frame rate affects how the driving feels as well. Rockstar confirmed before launch that its target is 30 fps on every console, PS5 Pro included.

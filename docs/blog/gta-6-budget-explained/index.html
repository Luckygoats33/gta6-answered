GTA 6 Budget: What It Cost to Make, and Why Nobody Knows

Published Nov 15, 2026. Facts checked Nov 16, 2026.

Nobody outside Take-Two knows how much money was spent on GTA 6 development. Take-Two and Rockstar have never published a budget, and the most they’ve said on the record is that it was expensive. The numbers you see online, usually $1 billion, $1.5 billion or $2 billion, are analyst estimates and internet lore. Here’s where each one comes from, and why the honest answer is still "a lot, exact figure unknown."

What Take-Two has said

Very little, on purpose. In a Business Insider interview reported in May 2026, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick declined to give a figure. His one-line answer amounted to "it was expensive." That’s it. No dollar amount, no range, nothing in an earnings report that breaks out GTA 6’s cost.

That’s normal. Publishers almost never disclose what a single game costs. Take-Two reports its finances as a whole company, with games, marketing and running costs spread across many lines. You can’t pull "the GTA 6 budget" out of its quarterly filings, even if you’re very good at spreadsheets.

What Take-Two has been happy to talk about is the money it expects to make. In May 2026 it guided for net bookings of $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion for its fiscal year ending March 2027, roughly 20% growth, with GTA 6 as the centerpiece. That tells you how big the bet is, but not what the bet cost.

Where the big numbers come from

Three figures get repeated the most:

  • $1 billion to $1.5 billion. This is the most grounded estimate. It comes from a Business Insider analysis based on industry analysts, reported in May 2026. It’s still an outside estimate, and it’s not clear whether it covers development only or development plus marketing.
  • $2 billion. This one has messier roots. One version traces back to an unverified claim that circulated after the 2022 Rockstar leak, from someone who admitted having no documents to back it up. Another comes from a venture capital analyst who used a $2 billion investment figure to model break-even timelines. Neither is a documented cost.
  • $3 billion and up. These show up in newsletters and video essays that count six or more years of payroll across a very large studio. Interesting arithmetic, not evidence.

If a headline says "GTA 6 cost $2 billion" as fact, it’s skipping a step. "Analysts estimate" is the honest framing.

Why it’s plausibly huge anyway

You don’t need a leaked spreadsheet to see why the cost is big. A few things Rockstar has said on the record:

  • The studio grew. Rob Nelson, Rockstar’s head of development, told Dazed that Rockstar has more than doubled in size since Red Dead Redemption 2.
  • The research was deep. The team spent years in Miami and the surrounding area, meeting all kinds of real-life figures for reference.
  • The detail is enormous. More than 50 street artists designed murals, and Rockstar built new tools to create its most varied population of characters yet.
  • It took a long time. GTA V came out in 2013. GTA 6 comes out on November 19, 2026, after two delays, the last of them announced in November 2025.

Payroll for a very large team over many years, plus licensed music, motion capture and a global marketing push, gets expensive fast. The exact number is unknown. The scale isn’t.

How much did GTA 6 actually cost to make compared to GTA V?

GTA V’s figure is also an estimate. A widely repeated 2013 newspaper report put it at about $265 million for development and marketing combined. Rockstar never confirmed that either.

So the comparison is estimate versus estimate. If the $1 billion to $1.5 billion range is close, GTA 6 cost somewhere around four to six times GTA V’s reported figure, before you adjust for a decade of inflation. Treat that as a rough sense of scale, not a fact.

Why it matters for players

Mostly, it doesn’t change your game. But it does explain a few things:

  • The price. Take-Two and Rockstar confirmed $79.99 for Standard and $99.99 for Ultimate in the US. Big budgets push publishers toward higher prices and premium editions. See the price page.
  • The delays. When a project costs this much, a publisher would rather slip a date than ship it unfinished. When the game slipped to November 2026, Zelnick said moving a date is painful but Take-Two has never regretted doing it in hindsight.
  • Online. Rockstar hasn’t dated GTA 6 Online, but long-running online modes are how big games keep earning for years. Our online page tracks what’s actually been announced.

The budget question will probably stay unanswered. If Take-Two ever does disclose it, it’ll be in an earnings call or an interview, and we’ll add it to the news page. Until then, be skeptical of anyone who sounds certain.

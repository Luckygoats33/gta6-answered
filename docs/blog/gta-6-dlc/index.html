Will GTA 6 Get Story DLC? What Rockstar Has Said

Published Nov 16, 2026.

Rockstar hasn’t announced any GTA 6 DLC, story expansion or map expansion as of November 16, 2026, and nothing it has said ahead of the November 19 launch promises one. The only paid add-on on sale is the Ultimate upgrade, which unlocks 16 extra items for Standard owners. Rockstar’s history points both ways: GTA IV got two big story episodes, while GTA V’s planned story DLC was cancelled as GTA Online took off.

What Rockstar has said about GTA 6 story DLC

Nothing official. Rockstar’s pre-launch material talks about the main game, the editions and the pre-order bonus. It doesn’t mention story expansions, new cities, or a season pass. Rockstar also described the launch as a single-player experience and hasn’t announced GTA 6 Online.

If Rockstar does plan a GTA 6 expansion, it will announce it on the Rockstar Newswire. Anything else you read, including "insider" posts about new cities arriving as DLC, is a rumor until then. Our rumors page tracks the ones that keep coming back.

The Ultimate upgrade isn’t story DLC

People sometimes call the Ultimate Edition upgrade "DLC," and technically it’s downloadable content. But it’s not new story. Rockstar listed what it adds, and it’s all items and places inside the existing game:

  • Vehicles like the ’95 Grotti Cheetah and a ’67 Vapid Dominator Buggy.
  • Engraved weapons and his-and-hers revolvers.
  • Outfits, tattoos and a hair salon.
  • Ultimate-only shops, including two mod shops and a tattoo parlor.
  • A gang compound raid and a classic car commission.

Some of those are small side activities, but none is a new storyline. Xbox lists the upgrade at $20, as reported by Forbes. See our upgrade page for how to buy it.

What GTA IV did: real story expansions

GTA IV is the best case for hope. In 2009 Rockstar released two full story expansions, The Lost and Damned and The Ballad of Gay Tony, each with a new lead character and story set in the same Liberty City. They were later bundled as Episodes from Liberty City.

Red Dead Redemption got the same treatment with Undead Nightmare in 2010, a standalone zombie story in the same world.

What GTA V did: story DLC that never came

GTA V is the case against. Rockstar worked on single-player expansions, and then stopped. The best-known one is Agent Trevor, a spy story for Trevor Philips.

  • Trevor’s actor, Steven Ogg, has said he recorded material for it before it was dropped.
  • Joe Rubino, a former Rockstar camera artist, said in a YouTube interview that it was about half done when it was cancelled. He said GTA Online had become so profitable that a story expansion was hard to justify.
  • Rockstar co-founder Dan Houser later said the DLC was "half-done," and suggested that if it had shipped, the team probably wouldn’t have gotten to make Red Dead Redemption 2.

Red Dead Redemption 2 followed the same pattern. It got Red Dead Online and no story expansion.

So the last two Rockstar games skipped story DLC entirely, and the money went into the online modes instead.

What that means for GTA 6

The honest answer is that nobody outside Rockstar knows. A few things are worth weighing:

  • GTA 6 Online hasn’t launched. Bloomberg has reported 2027 for multiplayer, which Rockstar hasn’t confirmed. If Online follows GTA V’s path, it’s likely to get most of Rockstar’s post-launch attention.
  • The map is huge already. Rockstar called Leonida its biggest world by a wide margin, and said the story won’t take you everywhere. A map expansion would be adding to the largest map Rockstar has built.
  • Houser has left Rockstar. The co-founder who wrote much of the series left in 2020, so his views don’t necessarily reflect how the studio works now.

None of that rules out story DLC. It just means you shouldn’t buy anything expecting it.

GTA 6 map DLC and expansions: what to expect

If you see a "GTA 6 map DLC" or "Liberty City expansion" announcement, check where it came from. Real announcements come from Rockstar’s own channels. Fake ones spread through edited screenshots and YouTube thumbnails. Never enter your account details on a site promising early access to an expansion.

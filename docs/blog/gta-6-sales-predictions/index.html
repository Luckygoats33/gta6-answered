GTA 6 Sales: Predictions, Records and What Take-Two Expects

Published Nov 15, 2026. Facts checked Nov 16, 2026.

GTA 6 sales will be enormous by any measure, but the only numbers that count are the ones Take-Two publishes, and as of November 16, 2026 the game hasn’t launched yet. Everything so far is guidance, pre-order commentary and analyst predictions. Here’s what each one says, clearly labeled, so you can tell an estimate from a result.

GTA 6 comes out on November 19, 2026. Once Take-Two announces sales figures, those will beat every prediction below.

What Take-Two expects

Take-Two, Rockstar’s parent company, doesn’t give unit forecasts for single games. It gives company-wide money forecasts, and GTA 6 sits at the center of them.

From its November 9, 2026 results for the quarter that ended September 30:

  • Full fiscal year (to March 31, 2027): net bookings of $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion, unchanged from earlier guidance.
  • October to December 2026 quarter: net bookings of $3.27 billion to $3.42 billion, roughly double the previous quarter. That’s the quarter that captures GTA 6’s launch.
  • Pre-orders: Take-Two said GTA 6 pre-orders were tracking "far in excess of our initial expectations."
  • Spending: operating expenses for that quarter were expected to rise 21%, mostly from GTA 6 marketing.

Net bookings is Take-Two’s measure of what it sold in a period. It covers every game the company publishes, not just GTA 6, so you can’t turn it straight into a copies figure.

GTA 6 sales predictions from named analysts

These are estimates, made before launch. None is a sales figure. GamesRadar+ collected these after the price was announced in June 2026:

  • David Cole, DFC Intelligence: about 25 million day-one sales, mostly pre-orders.
  • Serkan Toto, Kantan Games: thought 30 million units at launch was possible.
  • Joost van Dreunen, NYU Stern: at least 38 million copies and just over $3 billion in sales in the first 12 months.

Two of them also sounded a note of caution. Cole thinks GTA 6 will be stronger up front but isn’t sure it can match GTA V’s long lifespan, partly because it’s launching on only two console families. Van Dreunen expects lower long-term unit sales than GTA V, but higher spending per player.

Other estimates float around online with bigger numbers. Unless a named analyst or firm stands behind them, we leave them out.

The records GTA 6 is chasing

GTA V set the bar, and it’s a high one:

  • Launch: GTA V sold about 11 million copies on its first day in 2013, per GamesRadar+, and passed $1 billion in sales within three days.
  • Lifetime: Take-Two says GTA V has sold nearly 235 million units worldwide, up five million in a single quarter, more than 13 years after release.
  • Online: Bloomberg called GTA Online the biggest single factor in those lifetime sales.

So GTA 6 sales in 24 hours can plausibly beat GTA V’s day one, since the predictions above start at more than double it. Beating GTA V’s lifetime total is a much longer race, and it may depend on things Rockstar hasn’t announced yet, like an online mode and other platforms.

GTA 6 sales so far: where real numbers come from

When you see a "GTA 6 sold X million" headline, check where it came from. In order of trust:

  1. Take-Two or Rockstar announcements. A press release or a statement on an earnings call. This is the real number.
  2. Take-Two’s quarterly results. The report covering October to December 2026 will be the first one that includes the launch. These reports show money and sometimes units.
  3. Market trackers like Circana in the US. Useful for rankings, but they cover specific markets and channels, not worldwide totals.
  4. Third-party estimates from app and store trackers. Estimates, often from partial data.
  5. Social media posts with no source. Ignore them.

Watch for the difference between sold-in (copies shipped to stores and sold digitally) and sell-through (copies bought by players). Publishers often report sold-in, and it isn’t the same thing.

Why it matters to players

Sales numbers aren’t just for investors. A huge launch usually means longer support, more updates and more confidence in big extras, like an online mode or new platforms. None of that is announced for GTA 6, and our online page and PC page track the status. But history says a record-breaking Rockstar game doesn’t get left alone for long.
